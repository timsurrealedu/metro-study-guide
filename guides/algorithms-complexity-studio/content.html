Optimistic ceilings & pruning rule

Name it Branch-and-Bound (Fractional Upper Bound) Pretend remaining bag space can take fractional slices of the next richest item to get a guaranteed ceiling—if even that ceiling loses to your record, skip the branch.
Example
Once items 1 + 2 earn $110 for real, skipping item 1 caps out at items 2 + 3 = $90. Since 90 ≤ 110, you never need to explore that branch.
Formal Upper-Bound Formula

Bound formula: ub = v_{\text{so far}} + \text{frac}(W_{\text{rem}}). You may prune only when ub \le best\_so\_far—never prune using current integer profit v_{\text{so far}} alone.

Pruning on current integer profit instead of upper bound
A node with v_{\text{so far}} = 0 can still reach 110 later; you may only prune when the optimistic fractional ub \le best\_so\_far.

Reduction direction + 4-step proof template

Name it Polynomial-Time Reduction (A \le_{P} B) Always translate FROM the known-hard puzzle A INTO your target puzzle B—never the reverse!
4-Step NP-Completeness Exam Proof Template

Check off all four mandatory steps whenever proving a language B is NP-complete on an exam.

  1. Step 1 · B ∈ NPState the certificate and polynomial verifier

    Define a poly-size witness y and show a deterministic algorithm verifies (x, y) in O(n^{k}) time.

  2. Step 2 · Pick Source AChoose a known NPC problem A (A \le_{P} B)

    Never reduce B \le_{P} A! Pick \text{3-SAT}, \text{VERTEX-COVER}, or \text{HAM-CYCLE} to match B's structure.

  3. Step 3 · Construct F(x)Describe the polynomial-time reduction mapping

    Show how to build instance F(x) from x in O(n^{k}) time and space (gadgets, thresholds).

  4. Step 4 · Two-Way ProofProve both x \in A \Rightarrow F(x) \in B and F(x) \in B \Rightarrow x \in A

    Forward turns a solution of A into a witness for B; backward extracts a valid solution of A from B.

Reducing B ≤ₚ A in the wrong direction
Showing B \le_{P} A only proves B is at most as hard as A. To prove B is hard, reduce known hard A \le_{P} B.
